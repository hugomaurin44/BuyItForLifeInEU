# BIFL Guide: Buying sustainable and accessible

This guide lists products that last, are easily repaired, and remain affordable. The objective is not to sell unattainable luxury, but to highlight what lasts with good value for money. Modular, simple materials (metal, solid wood, thick cotton) are highlighted.

This guide is based on the numerous posts on r/BuyItForLife and the reputation of the brands. we have no affiliation with these brands.

### Indicator legend

(Price | Repairability | Duration)

## Pitfalls to avoid

Some once-famous brands have seen their quality plummet. To avoid or buy with caution:

* **The North Face** general public (only the Summit/Steep Tech lines remain correct)
* **Dr. Martens** Made in Asia (prefer Made in England only)
* **Timberland** (collapsed quality, avoid completely)
* **Coleman** recent (vintage quality no longer exists)
* **Beats by Dre** (catastrophic after-sales service, rapid obsolescence)
* “Gaming RGB” peripherals (prefer professional versions)

## Computing and storage

### Desktop computers

Always more durable, 100% modular. Graphics card (prefer AMD), RAM, hard drive can be changed.

Mini-computer for servers/projects. Nearly indestructible, minimal consumption, huge community. Not for classic office use.
($ | ★★☆ | 10+ years)

### Laptops

[**Lenovo ThinkPad**](https://www.lenovo.com/us/en/c/laptops/thinkpad/) (T and X series) 🏆
Professional standard. Excellent keyboard, sturdy chassis, relatively simple repair. The reconditioned market is huge, so parts and guides abundant.
($$-$$$ | ★★★ | 8-12 years old)

The only modern, fully modular laptop. Motherboard, screen, USB ports can be changed as needed. Strong commitment to repairability. High price but long term investment.

[**MacBook Air**](https://www.apple.com/macbook-air/) M1/M2
No fan therefore less dust and mechanical breakdowns. Huge battery life, very good performance for typical use.
⚠️ Warning: RAM welded, battery stuck, repair almost impossible. To be considered as a definitive long-term purchase, not as a repairable product.
($$$ | ☆☆☆ | 8-12 years old)

## Where to buy second-hand

The second-hand market is essential for the BIFL philosophy. Many older products are better built than their modern equivalents.

### General platforms

* [**Leboncoin**](https://www.leboncoin.fr) / **Facebook Marketplace**: All types of products. Check condition in person. Negotiation possible.
* [**Vinted**](https://www.vinted.fr): Clothing, bags, shoes. Beware of counterfeits on premium brands.
* **eBay**: Ideal for spare parts, rare models, vintage electronics.

### Refurbished electronics

* [**BackMarket**](https://www.backmarket.fr): Smartphones, computers, tablets. Refurbished with 12-24 month warranty. Fair prices.
* **Largo** (ex-Recommerce): French competitor to BackMarket.

### Computer hardware

* [**Reddit r/HardwareSwap**](https://www.reddit.com/r/hardwareswap/): PC components between enthusiasts. Fair prices, serious community (especially USA/UK).
* **French hardware forums** ([**Dealabs**](https://www.dealabs.com/), [**HFR**](https://forum.hardware.fr/)): Active classifieds sections.

### Furniture and household objects

* **Emmaüs / Ressourceries**: Furniture, crockery, books. Very low price, solidarity approach.
* [**Selency**](https://www.selency.fr) / **Vinterior**: Vintage and mid-century. More expensive but qualitative selection.

### Technical clothing

* [**Worn Wear (Patagonia)**](https://wornwear.patagonia.com/): Officially refurbished with warranty. Buyback program.
* **Specialized workwear flea markets**: Carhartt, vintage Dickies often better constructed than new.

### Bikes

* [**Leboncycle**](https://www.leboncycle.fr): Platform specializing in used bikes with verification.
* **Associative bicycle workshops**: Recycling, spare parts, community repair.

### Tools

* **flea markets and garage sales**: Old German/French tools (Facom, Usag) often superior to modern ones.
* **Specialized Facebook groups**: "Old tools", "Workshop and tools" etc.

General advice: For electronics, choose reconditioned with warranty. For the rest (furniture, tools, clothing), second-hand is often more interesting.

Guide written with the ambition to point out what really lasts, without marketing compromise.
